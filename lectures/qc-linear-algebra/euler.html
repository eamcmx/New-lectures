Rotation, not explosion

For real inputs, ex blows up. Feed it an imaginary number and something entirely different happens: eiθ is a point that rotates. Its velocity i·eiθ is always perpendicular to its position, so it can never move outward — it's doomed to circle at distance 1 forever. Any complex number is then z = r·eiθ: a length and a direction.

De Moivre = n rotations of θ each

Notice how each multiplication by z just adds another angle θ — no trig identities needed. That's all (eiθ)n = einθ = cos nθ + i sin nθ is saying: raising to the n-th power means rotating by θ, n times in a row. Angles add; magnitudes multiply (and 1ⁿ = 1).

Why quantum phases live on this circle

A qubit amplitude α can be multiplied by a phase eiθ, and yet |eiθα|² = |α|² — the measurement probability doesn't budge, because eiθ has length exactly 1. This is why phases in quantum mechanics live on the unit circle: they rotate amplitudes without stretching them, invisible alone but decisive in interference.
