4 · Does it preserve norms?

A faint unit circle with 12 unit vectors. Apply the current matrix: unitaries land the circle exactly on itself; everything else stretches it.

Honesty note: when A has complex entries, Av leaves the real plane, so for those matrices we draw each direction v at radius ‖Av‖ — the stretch profile on the real slice. For real matrices we draw the true image Av. Either way, unitary ⇒ every radius stays 1.

The dagger is bookkeeping for bra-ket

Notice how conjugate + transpose is exactly what makes ⟨u|A†|v⟩ = ⟨v|A|u⟩* work: bras and kets trade places, and the stray conjugate from flipping ⟨v|u⟩ = ⟨u|v⟩* is absorbed by conjugating the entries. That's why (AB)† = B†A† reverses the order — the bra reads it from the other side.

Why quantum insists on these species

Gates must be unitary — reversible and norm-preserving, so probabilities keep summing to 1. Observables must be Hermitian — their eigenvalues are real, and a measurement must read out a real number. Measurements themselves are projectors: apply one twice and nothing new happens, P² = P.

Memorize the picture, not five definitions

The zoo is nested: positive ⊂ Hermitian ⊂ normal, unitary ⊂ normal, projector ⊂ positive. One picture carries all the facts — Hermitian ⇒ real eigenvalues, unitary ⇒ eigenvalues on the unit circle, projector ⇒ eigenvalues 0 or 1, and normal is precisely "diagonalizable with an orthonormal eigenbasis".
