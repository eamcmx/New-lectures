Why prompts matter the PAAL idea

The goal of using an AI in this course is not answers — it is calibrated practice: problems pitched just above your level, with your reasoning checked step by step. A good prompt turns the AI into a tutor that asks one question at a time and waits for you to commit; a lazy prompt turns it into a homework printer that quietly does the learning for you. Use the traffic light below to keep yourself honest about which mode you're in.

🟢 AI as coach Generate practice on ⟨u|v⟩, eigenvalues, Kronecker products; have it check your reasoning and probe your understanding. This is where learning happens.
🟡 AI as reference Definitions, decoding notation ("what does Σ|j⟩⟨j| = I mean?"). Fine — but verify every claim against the slides before you trust it.
🔴 Avoid: AI as answer machine Pasting homework verbatim and copying the output = skill atrophy. You will meet the exam alone, and it does not accept screenshots of a chat.

The verification habit non-negotiable

  • ✓Re-derive one AI-given fact per session against the slides. If the AI says Hermitian operators have real eigenvalues, find that statement in the lecture and check the 2×2 case yourself.
  • ✓Ask the AI to argue the opposite. "Now convince me the eigenvalues of a projector could be 2." Whatever survives the counter-argument is what you actually understand.
  • ✓Numbers are checkable — check them. If it hands you a qubit state α|0⟩ + β|1⟩, compute |α|² + |β|² yourself. If it isn't 1, the "state" was fiction, however fluent the paragraph around it.
  • ✓Cross-check with the interactives in this set: drag the vectors on the eigen page, multiply the matrices on the operators page. Pixels don't hallucinate.

Protocol beats content

Notice how every strong prompt here specifies an interaction protocol — one question at a time, commit before reveal. That single rule is what turns a chat into practice: it forces retrieval, and retrieval is where memory forms.

Fluency ≠ truth

AI explanations are exactly as fluent when they're wrong. A confident paragraph about a "unitary" matrix that doesn't satisfy U†U = I reads just like a correct one. This is why the verification habit exists — and why the interactive pages here are your ground truth.

Raise difficulty, not comfort

The best students use AI to increase difficulty — "make the matrix complex", "add a proof step", "two right in a row means harder" — not to decrease effort. The struggle you're tempted to outsource is the product.
