Tensor & Kronecker Product — spaces that multiply

Edit the blue matrix A and the yellow matrix B, then hover the big result to trace every entry back to the pair that produced it. Below, build two-qubit kets from one-qubit pieces — and meet the states that refuse to be built that way.

When two quantum systems live together — two particles, two qubits — their joint home is not V next to W but the tensor product V ⊗ W. Its basis is every pair |vi⟩⊗|wj⟩ of basis kets, so an m-dimensional space times an n-dimensional one gives dimension m·n. Notation shrinks along the way: |u⟩⊗|v⟩ = |u,v⟩ = |uv⟩, and |ψ⟩⊗|ψ⟩⊗…⊗|ψ⟩ (k copies) is written |ψ⟩⊗k.

The product is bilinear — linear in each slot separately: (α|u⟩+|u′⟩)⊗|v⟩ = α|u⟩⊗|v⟩ + |u′⟩⊗|v⟩. Operators come along for the ride via (A⊗B)(|u⟩⊗|v⟩) = A|u⟩ ⊗ B|v⟩ — and as a matrix, A⊗B is the Kronecker product: for A of size m×n and B of size p×q, the result is the mp×nq block matrix where each entry aij becomes the block aij·B.

4 · Dimensions multiply: n qubits → 2n

One qubit lives in 2 dimensions. Each extra qubit tensors on another factor of 2 — the joint space is ℂ²⊗ℂ²⊗…⊗ℂ² = ℂ2ⁿ. Slide n and watch the space explode.

n = 3  →  dim = 23 = 8

Describing 10 qubits classically already takes 2¹⁰ = 1024 complex amplitudes; 300 qubits would take more amplitudes than there are atoms in the observable universe. This exponential wall is exactly where quantum computing's power hides.

⊗ multiplies, ⊕ would add

A direct sum of two qubit spaces would give 2+2 = 4… but grow linearly forever after. The tensor product gives 2·2 = 4 and then keeps multiplying: dimensions compound. Two qubits genuinely live in a 4-dimensional space where every pair of basis kets — |00⟩, |01⟩, |10⟩, |11⟩ — is its own direction.

The block pattern is bilinearity

Notice how the Kronecker result is nothing but "copy B, scale by aij, place in block ij". That is bilinearity made visible: scaling A scales every block, scaling B scales inside every block — linear in each factor separately, exactly as (A⊗B)(|u⟩⊗|v⟩) = A|u⟩⊗B|v⟩ demands.

What doesn't factor is entangled

The product states |u⟩⊗|v⟩ are a thin slice of the full tensor space — the slice where ad = bc. Everything outside it, like the Bell state, is entangled: the whole carries information that no description of the parts can hold. This is why the tensor product, not the humble list-of-parts, is the true stage of multi-particle quantum mechanics.
